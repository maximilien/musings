## LinkedIn Post

New post: **Value of Hackathons: What I Learned from a Dozen+ Hackathons in 2025**

Hackathons are one of the best ways to innovate. But after doing over a dozen in 2025, I've come to see them as something else entirely: accidental research labs.

You don't just build things at hackathons—you learn how other people build things. What tools they reach for. What strategies work under pressure. What shortcuts break. If you're paying attention, that observational data is as valuable as anything you code.

The post covers:

- Why hackathons are uniquely useful research environments (time compression, diverse participants, real motivation, low-stakes experimentation)
- What companies actually get out of sponsoring hackathons (hint: it's product research, not generosity)
- My full playbook—before, during, and at presentation time
- Perspectives from Dave Nielsen (PayPal's first Developer Advocate, 1000+ events), Adam Chan (hackersquad.io), and Allie Jones (multi-hackathon winner)
- Lessons learned the hard way from a year of hacking

One insight from Allie that stuck with me: "The entry point moved from 'can you code fast enough' to 'do you care enough to try.'"

AI tools have fundamentally changed what's possible in 24 hours. What used to require a team of five now takes one or two people with the right tools. The barrier to building something real has never been lower.

My advice: find a hackathon this month and just show up. You'll learn more in a weekend than in a month of tutorials.

Read the full post: https://open.substack.com/pub/maximilien/p/value-of-hackathons-what-i-learned

Thanks to Dave Nielsen, Adam Chan, and Allie Jones for sharing their perspectives.
