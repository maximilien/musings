## LinkedIn Post

New post: **Selecting the Right Software Stack**

The old advice: "Use the right tool for the job."

The new advice: describe the job precisely, let an AI agent recommend the tool, and verify the reasoning.

Most developers still pick a stack based on what they know—then use AI agents to code in it. I think the order should flip:

1. Describe your requirements (performance, scale, team, deployment)
2. Ask an AI coding agent to recommend a stack and justify it
3. Challenge the reasoning—then decide

Your role is shifting from stack selector to requirements articulator and decision verifier.

The post covers why this matters, with war stories from Twitter's Rails journey, Cloud Foundry's rewrite from Ruby to Go, and my own work on AI agent infrastructure. Plus a comparison framework for Python, Go, Rust, Java/Kotlin, and TypeScript.

One insight that stuck with me: programming languages are beginning to hold the same relationship to natural language that assembly holds to high-level code. Still essential under the hood—increasingly invisible to the person directing the work.

Thanks to Nima Kaviani and Dmitriy Kalinin for pushing the first draft in a better direction.

Read the full post: https://open.substack.com/pub/maximilien/p/selecting-the-right-software-stack
